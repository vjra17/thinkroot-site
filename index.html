The gap

Students are taught how to perform. Rarely how to stay steady when things get hard.

School teaches knowledge. Traditional activities develop specific abilities. Yet students are rarely taught how to manage the intense pressure, negative self-talk, and frustration that can come with them.

A student can be capable, motivated, and well supported—and still struggle to use what they know when a test feels overwhelming, an assignment becomes difficult, a mistake shakes their confidence, or emotions take over. ThinkRoot fills that gap.

Why It Matters

The missing layer behind academics, sports, and confidence.

Families invest deeply in tutoring, sports, music, and enrichment. These opportunities matter. But a student’s ability to benefit from them also depends on how they handle stress, impulses, and discouragement in the moment.

ThinkRoot does not compete with these activities. It builds the foundational skill set that strengthens a student’s ability to benefit from all of them.

Knowledge matters. So does the capacity to use it when things get difficult.

ThinkRoot builds that capacity through science-informed teaching, research-backed practices, reflection, and real-world application.

What Makes ThinkRoot's Resilience Lab Different

The Science of Root-Cause Thinking

ThinkRoot goes beneath the visible reaction. In age-appropriate language, students examine the interacting biological, psychological, and behavioral patterns underneath—thoughts, emotions, body signals, interpretations, pressures, and habits—then learn to choose a more constructive response.

Students explore the science behind these patterns: how the brain and body respond to stress, how digital environments compete for attention, and how repeated choices strengthen habits over time. Through research-backed practices, they learn to pause, reset, refocus, recover, and decide what to do next.

That is the idea behind ThinkRoot: do not stop at what happened. Understand what shaped it—and build the capacity to respond differently.

How It Works

A repeatable method for real-world pressure.

ThinkRoot teaches a clear, practical process students can return to again and again. The goal is not to give them more information. It is to equip them with a usable method for handling stress, mistakes, digital distractions, and everyday challenges.

🧠
Understand
Students learn how their brain, body signals, thoughts, and impulses directly impact their performance and focus.
🔄
Practice
Students build concrete tools through age-appropriate activities, reflection, and low-stakes repetition until the skills feel natural.
🚀
Apply
Students confidently use those tools in real-time—during tough tests, after sports mistakes, in friendships, and at home.

Meet the founder

Tina Puri

Founder & Program Director

Tina Puri is the Founder and Program Director of ThinkRoot Young Leaders, a Wellness Educator and Coach, and a Senior Peer Counselor for the cities of Fremont, Union City, and Newark. She brings more than 22 years of experience as a software professional together with extensive work in wellness education, youth development, and community leadership.

Her work with young people began through years of involvement in schools and community programs while her children were growing up. About 15 years ago, a personal health challenge led her into a deeper exploration of wellness, mindfulness, and the mind-body connection, gradually reshaping the direction of her work.

Since then, Tina has founded and led student well-being initiatives across elementary, middle, and high-school settings, including stress-management, yoga, and gratitude programs. She has mentored students through HOSA – Future Health Professionals, speech and debate across Fremont Unified School District, Stanford Challenge Success, and Scout programs.

Since 2019, Tina has also worked with adults and seniors through group programs, online sessions, and individual wellness coaching, including a weekly wellness group serving 40–45 seniors.

She co-founded Rewired to Inspire with Fremont high-school students, a four-year intergenerational initiative recognized by Tri-City Voice and Congressman Michael M. Honda.

ThinkRoot brings together her systems-thinking background, wellness education, and experience working across generations to help young people build the awareness, resilience, and judgment to meet life well.

“Life taught me that the skills we need most are often the ones we learn too late — how to know ourselves, master our minds, and live with purpose. ThinkRoot grew from a desire to give young people those tools earlier, while they are still becoming who they will be.”

Common Questions

Questions parents ask.

Who is this program for?
Fall 2026 Resilience Lab is designed for students in Grades 4–9. It is for capable students as well as those who are struggling. Families join because they want their child to have practical tools for pressure, setbacks, focus, confidence, habits, and everyday decisions.
My child is already doing well in school. Do they still need this?
Academic performance and the skills that support performance are not the same. A child can have strong grades and still shut down under pressure, give up after a mistake, or struggle with self-doubt. ThinkRoot develops the focus, regulation, recovery, and judgment students need when things get hard.
What will my child actually do in sessions?
Sessions are interactive and practical — not lectures. Children work through guided activities, reflection exercises, and discussion to build skills they can use immediately. Each session ends with a simple practice they can apply on their own between meetings.
Where are sessions held?
For Fall 2026, we are currently planning live Zoom sessions on Wednesdays, 3:30–4:30 PM PT | 6:30–7:30 PM ET. Families should still submit the enrollment form if that time does not work; the final schedule will be confirmed after family availability has been reviewed. Enrolled families will also receive details about the local Bay Area group experience connected with each enrolled series.
What happens after I submit a registration request?
ThinkRoot will review your request and follow up with next steps. Enrollment is confirmed after the family has received the program details and completed payment.
